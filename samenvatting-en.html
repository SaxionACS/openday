What is TI?

Almost everything around you contains a tiny computer: your bike light, the payment terminal, a car or a pacemaker. It measures something, decides and controls something. That is called an embedded system. At Technische Informatica (TI) you write the software that does this.

TI, HBO-ICT or Electrical Engineering?

Electrical EngineeringCircuits, circuit boards and signals. You want to know what goes on inside the chip and the wire.
Technische InformaticaCode for microcontrollers, robots and machines. You want to program and see something move.
HBO-ICTApps, websites and systems for companies. You mainly work with software, data and users.

What can you become?

Embedded software engineerSoftware inside a device. Graduation project: low-power firmware for a wearable heart monitor (Diplora).
Software engineerBuilding large systems in a team. Graduation project: real-time data in distribution centres (Amazon).
Robotics & automation engineerMaking robots and machines work on their own. Graduation project: an unmanned vehicle that recognises holes in the ground (Demcon).
AI & vision engineerTeaching cameras to understand what they see. Graduation project: recognising safety clothing on building sites (BauWatch).
Test engineerProving that software does what it should. Graduation project: bombarding software with strange input (Thales).
Security specialistKeeping devices and data safe. Graduation project: securing working memory for digital forensics (FIOD).

What will you work on? For example self-driving systems, medical technology, energy, security or robotics. In year 2 you choose an elective line: Embedded Systems, Intelligent Systems or Signal Processing Systems. After that come an internship, a minor and your graduation project.

To be honest

Year 1 is tough: more than 4 in 10 first-year students do not continue to year 2 of TI. That is why every first-year student has a fixed home group and a study coach who keeps an eye on them, so you know in time where you stand.

Can I do it?

Admission

  • havo with N&T, N&G or E&M: direct admission. With C&M: with maths A or B.
  • vwo: with any profile.
  • mbo 4: with any mbo level 4 programme.

These are Dutch diplomas. Any additional requirements, also for international diplomas, are on saxion.nl.

What is hard?

  • The pace. Programming moves fast from week 1. You need to practise every week.
  • Planning yourself. About 17 hours of classes and 23 hours of self-study per week.
  • Maths and electronics, especially if you have not done them for a while. Ask for help in time.

A few myths

"You need to be able to program already."In Programming 1 you simply start from zero.
"You need to be a maths genius."You learn to apply maths. Perseverance counts more than talent.
"You sit still behind a screen all day."You solder, measure, test and make robots drive.
"AI will take over this work."AI writes code. Someone has to understand what happens inside the chip.
